**Purpose:** Compare current Allog USA provider pricing against direct Steamship Line (SSL) inland / drayage pricing for the highest-volume 2026 lanes that have a sufficiently comparable routing basis.

## Executive Summary

The current management comparison contains **10 selected comparable lanes** after removing lanes where the SSL service does not provide a defensible apples-to-apples routing basis.

Across the selected lanes:

- **Allog's actual commercial rate is below the SSL average on 8 of 10 lanes.**
- **Allog's actual commercial rate beats the lowest available SSL on 6 of 10 lanes.**
- After allowing for the additional operational services included in Allog All-in packages, the **indicative 2-day-equivalent range is below the SSL average on all 10 selected lanes.**
- The selected portfolio represents **392 x 40' containers YTD in 2026**.
- At current actual commercial rates, the modeled Allog cost is approximately **USD 94,836 below the SSL-average benchmark**, or **15.2% lower**.
- Using the indicative normalization range, the estimated portfolio advantage increases to approximately **USD 129,546 to USD 146,986**, equivalent to roughly **20.8% to 23.6% below the SSL-average benchmark**.

The largest current contribution is **Oakland → Verdi, NV**, where Allog is USD 501.50 below the SSL average per container and the lane has 140 containers YTD.

## Allog All-in vs. 2-Day Normalization Method

Allog commonly requests and sells **All-in provider packages because clients solicit the service on that basis**. The normal All-in package in this analysis includes:

- 2 days chassis
- prepull
- 1 day storage

Although standalone reference values are approximately USD 150 for prepull and USD 50 per storage day, providers frequently price these services as a **commercial package**. Therefore, the actual difference between an All-in rate and a 2-day-chassis-only rate is not always USD 200. Depending on the provider / lane, the difference can be closer to approximately **USD 120, USD 150, USD 175, or USD 200**.

For this reason, the report does **not** present one fixed normalized price. Instead, for standard All-in lanes it uses:

> **Indicative 2-day equivalent = All-in rate less approximately USD 120 to USD 200**

This creates a range and avoids overstating savings based on an accessorial arithmetic assumption that may not reflect the provider's actual packaged pricing.

### Hatfield exception

Hatfield uses an enhanced package of **3 days chassis + 2 days storage + prepull**. Relative to the normal package, this adds one chassis day and one storage day. For management analysis, the indicative adjustment to a 2-day-equivalent basis is therefore estimated at approximately **USD 215 to USD 295**, producing the range shown in the table.

### Actual 2-day lanes

Where a genuine 2-day provider rate exists — currently Verdi, Lebanon, and Sandston in this report — the actual provider rate is used directly and no normalization is applied.

## Lane Selection Adjustments / Footnotes

### Portland, ME — omitted from numerical Top 10 comparison

Portland remains a high-volume Allog lane, but there is currently **no comparable SSL benchmark on the same New York routing basis**:

- Maersk: no current service on the comparison basis
- MSC: no current service
- Hapag-Lloyd: delivery is not performed directly through the same New York routing basis

Because there is no defensible current SSL average, Portland is excluded from the numerical Allog-vs-SSL Top 10 analysis rather than assigning an artificial value.

### Eau Claire, WI — omitted due to rail-ramp mismatch

Allog's historical delivery flow is **Chicago Ramp → Eau Claire**. Current SSL routing does not align:

- Maersk: Minneapolis Ramp
- Hapag-Lloyd: Minneapolis Ramp
- MSC: very likely Saint Paul Ramp; final confirmation pending

A comparison between Chicago-based Allog drayage and Minneapolis / Saint Paul SSL drayage would not be a true same-ramp comparison. Eau Claire is therefore excluded from the management benchmark.

### Replacement lanes

- **Savannah → Fort Mill, SC** — 16 containers YTD; clean comparable SSL pricing available from Maersk and MSC.
- **Los Angeles → Ontario, CA** — 16 containers YTD; all three SSL rates are available and the routing is directly comparable.

Baltimore → Baltimore had previously been removed from the selected comparison and replaced by **Oakland → Sacramento, CA**.

## Management Takeaways

1. **Allog is competitive on the actual commercial packages, not only after normalization.** Actual Allog pricing is below the SSL average on 8 of the 10 selected lanes.
2. **Allog beats the cheapest available SSL on 6 of 10 selected lanes using the actual commercial rate.**
3. **The service-package difference matters.** On lanes such as Denver and Sacramento, Allog's actual All-in price is above the SSL average, but the Allog rate includes additional operational services. The estimated 2-day-equivalent range is still below the SSL average on both lanes.
4. **The normalized result should be treated as a range, not a guaranteed quote.** Provider package economics mean accessorials cannot always be cleanly subtracted at standalone list prices.
5. **Verdi is the most financially material lane.** The combination of 140 containers YTD and a USD 501.50 actual per-container advantage versus the SSL average creates approximately USD 70,210 of modeled YTD advantage on that lane alone.
6. **Service availability is also a competitive factor.** Portland is excluded from the rate benchmark precisely because comparable SSL service is not currently available on the required routing basis; Allog's ability to provide the service remains operationally relevant even though it cannot be represented as a numerical SSL saving.
